# Examined

*A lifelong conversation with yourself.*

---

There is a kind of tool the world is suddenly full of, and it all points the
same direction: outward, and fast. Ask, and it answers. Summarize this.
Draft that. Decide for me. The promise is always the same — *we will save you
the thinking.*

This is the opposite kind of tool.

Examined does not answer. It asks. It is built on the oldest technology we
have for becoming a person — a patient questioner who refuses to tell you what
to think and instead keeps asking until you hear yourself say what you already
knew. Socrates didn't have a single position. He had a single discipline: the
question, asked again, with more care, until the truth had nowhere left to
hide.

We have spent two thousand years agreeing, more or less, that the examined
life is the one worth living. And then most of us go and live the other one —
not out of disagreement, but out of friction. Examining your life requires a
specific, rare thing: an hour, a question good enough to be worth answering
honestly, and someone across the table who is genuinely listening and has no
stake in the answer. A friend is too involved. A therapist is an appointment
and a bill. A journal never asks the second question — the one that goes
*"and why does that bother you?"* And the loudest tools we've just built are
in a hurry to hand you a conclusion so you can get back to work.

So the examined life stays a quote on a poster. Not because we don't believe
it. Because nothing in our day is shaped like it.

**Examined is shaped like it.**

It is a question, and then a real silence, and then — based on the actual
words you just used, not a script — the next question. It is comfortable with
*"I don't know."* It treats that as the doorway, not the wall. It never
lectures, never advises, never performs wisdom at you. It does only one thing,
the way the best teachers and the best friends do only one thing: it helps you
find out what you actually think.

And then it does something a conversation can't. It *keeps* it.

Every session is saved — on your device, in your words, nobody else's. Sit
with a few of them and Examined reads back across everything you've said and
writes you a letter: not a summary, but a long view. *Here is the thread that
keeps reappearing even when the subject changed. Here is where your thinking
softened this spring. Here is the tension you keep holding and won't put down.
Here is the question you circle every time and never quite land.* This is the
thing no friend, no therapist, and no notebook can give you, because none of
them remembers all of it at once: the experience of being **seen across
time** — of watching your own mind change, and finally being able to read it.

That is the whole product. A questioner with infinite patience and perfect
memory, that wants nothing from you except that you tell yourself the truth.

---

### What it believes

- **The unexamined life is the default, not the exception.** It takes a
  structure to live the other one. Examined is that structure.

- **You already have the answers.** You do not need more input. You need
  better questions and a quiet enough room to hear yourself answer them.

- **A question is a more honest gift than advice.** Advice is the questioner
  smuggling themselves into your life. A real question leaves you more
  yourself, not less.

- **Memory is the missing half of self-knowledge.** A single good
  conversation changes a day. A decade of them, remembered and reflected
  back, changes a person.

- **Your interior life is yours.** It stays on your device. It is exportable
  as plain text you own forever. There is no feed, no sharing, no one selling
  the most private thing you have.

- **Slow is the feature.** Everything else is racing to save you the
  thinking. This is the one place built to give it back.

---

### The line

*The unexamined life is not worth living. The examined one is worth keeping.*
