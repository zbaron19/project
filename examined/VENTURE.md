# Examined — the case for making this the work

This file is the part of the gift that takes the other part seriously. You
asked for the goal to be *the thing you'd pursue as a full-time job.* So here
is the honest argument for why this one could be — not a hype memo, a real
case, including where it's hard.

---

## Why this, and why you

You are a commercial real-estate lawyer who taught himself to ship an iOS app,
and who then built a private system — the playbook — whose emotional center
isn't the prompts or the legal clauses. It's the **reflection**: an AI reading
across everything you've made and writing you a letter about who you're
becoming. You built that for yourself, by hand, because no one had built it for
you. That's the tell. People build, unprompted, the thing they most wish
existed.

The instinct everyone (including the last model that wrote in your playbook)
keeps pushing you toward is *point your legal expertise at software.* It's good
advice. It's also a continuation of the life you're trying to leave — the same
domain, the same buyers, the same identity, just with better margins. It's a
better cage.

This is the other door. You studied **philosophy and economics** before you
studied law. Examined is the only idea in reach that uses *that* person — the
one who cared about the examined life before anyone was paying him by the hour
to stop. You are unusually well-suited to build it for three reasons that
rarely co-occur:

1. **You can ship it.** Solo. You've already done the hard, unglamorous version
   — App Store review, Sentry, the browser-direct-to-Claude pattern this very
   app reuses. The technical risk is near zero for you specifically.
2. **You have the philosophical seriousness it requires.** A tool like this
   dies the instant it feels like a life-coaching app or a horoscope. It lives
   or dies on the *quality of the questions* and the restraint of the voice.
   That's writing and judgment, not engineering — and it's exactly your taste.
3. **You are the user.** You are a high-earning professional quietly asking
   whether you're spending your one life right. That is the customer. You will
   never have to guess what they need.

## The category

Frame it cleanly so it doesn't get mistaken for what it isn't:

- **Not** a chatbot (those answer; this only asks).
- **Not** a journaling app (those are a blank page; this is a questioner with
  memory).
- **Not** a therapy or coaching app (no advice, no diagnosis, no credential
  risk, no "should").
- **Not** an AI companion / friend (no fake intimacy, no engagement loop, no
  pull to come back — the opposite: it wants you to leave having heard
  yourself).

The honest one-liner: **"A Socratic thinking partner that remembers."** The
category is *tools for the examined life* — deliberate-living software for
people who have the income and the ambition but have stopped asking what it's
for. That cohort is large, underserved by anything dignified, and exactly the
people who can pay.

## Why now

Three things only became true recently and all point the same way:

- Models got good enough to ask a genuinely *good* follow-up question from the
  exact words you used — the thing that was impossible to fake and is the whole
  product.
- The first wave of AI tools has been relentlessly *extractive of attention and
  productive of answers.* There is a real, growing hunger for the inverse — and
  almost no quality product serving it.
- Privacy as a *feature* (local-first, your-key, plain-text-you-own, nothing on
  our servers) is now a credible wedge, not a compromise. The most private
  thing a person has is their interior life; build the one product that
  visibly never touches it.

## How it makes money (and why the model fits the values)

The trap for a product like this is an engagement-maximizing subscription —
which would corrupt the entire premise. The model has to *reward you for being
done thinking,* not for scrolling. So:

- **Examined Annual ($80–120/yr).** Unlimited sessions; the *Long View*
  synthesis; the growing library of curated examinations; export and backup.
  Annual on purpose — this is a yearly practice, not a daily streak.
- **Bring-your-own-key, always free tier.** The local app works with your own
  Anthropic key for nothing. This is principled *and* the best possible funnel:
  the people who'll pay are the people who already felt it work.
- **The Year in Review ($40, once a year).** Around the new year, a long,
  beautifully typeset synthesis of your entire year of examination, printable /
  bindable. A keepsake of your own mind. This alone could be the wedge product.
- **Examined Circles (later).** Small, facilitated cohorts who run the same
  examination in the same week and meet to compare — a paid, premium social
  layer that never touches anyone's private text.
- **Licensed companion (later, optional).** The same engine, white-labeled for
  therapists, executive coaches, and spiritual directors to give clients
  *between sessions* — with the privacy posture as the selling point.

Rough math toward your number, conservatively: a tool with this little
competition, sold to professionals at ~$100/yr, needs on the order of
**4,000–5,000 paying members to clear $400K gross.** That is a small,
reachable number for a well-written product with a clear story and a founder
who can also *write the essays that sell it.* You don't need a hit. You need a
few thousand of the right people — and you happen to be one of them, so you
know where they are.

## The unfair advantage hiding in your closet

The hardest, slowest part of this business is **the questions and the voice** —
the curated examinations, the system prompt's restraint, the tone of the Long
View. Most builders would generate those with a model and ship slop. You've
spent a career writing precise, high-stakes language for a living, and a
lifetime quietly caring about these exact questions. The moat is *taste*, and
it's already yours. The 18 opening questions in the app are a first draft; the
real product is the hundred best questions a thoughtful person ever asked,
sequenced well. That's a body of work only someone like you makes well — and
it compounds, like the playbook did.

## A 90-day plan that fits a person with a day job

You don't quit anything. You moonlight, exactly like Path 1 in your own
transition note said to.

- **Weeks 1–2 — Use it on yourself.** Run one real examination every other day
  with the app as-is. You're the first customer and the editor; you'll feel
  every weak question. Rewrite them. This is product work disguised as living
  well.
- **Weeks 3–5 — Make it yours to ship.** Buy the domain, set the brand (the
  one in `/site` is a real starting point), wire up a hosted version with
  accounts + Stripe so non-technical people can pay without a key. You've done
  every piece of this before.
- **Weeks 6–9 — Write the thread you've never written.** The transition note
  told you to build a personal brand *before* you leave. This is the subject
  worth building it on, and it's not legal content you'd resent writing. One
  short essay a week on the examined life, the cost of the unexamined one, what
  you're learning from your own sessions. The essays *are* the marketing and
  the proof you can carry the voice.
- **Weeks 10–13 — Quiet launch to 100.** No big swing. Get it into the hands of
  100 thoughtful people, watch which questions make them go quiet, and ship the
  *Year in Review* as the first paid object. Let it teach you whether this is a
  product or a calling. Either answer is worth knowing.

## Where it's genuinely hard (so you go in clear-eyed)

- **Retention is the real risk.** A tool you're *supposed* to use slowly is a
  tool you can forget. The Long View and the Year in Review exist precisely to
  give a reason to return that isn't a manipulative streak. Watch this number
  honestly; it's the whole question.
- **It can drift into therapy.** It must never diagnose or advise. Hold that
  line ruthlessly — it's an ethical line first and a legal one second, and you
  of all people can hold a line in writing.
- **"Why pay for questions?"** The answer has to be *felt,* not argued. Which
  is why the free, bring-your-own-key tier isn't a giveaway — it's the proof.
  People pay for the memory, the synthesis, and the keepsake, not the chat.
- **It's small at first, and quiet.** This will not look like a rocket for a
  while. That's a feature for the kind of life you said you wanted, and a bug
  if you need a fast exit. Be honest with yourself about which you're
  optimizing for.

## The quiet reason

You can make a very good living continuing to be a lawyer who ships legal
tools. You probably will, and it's a fine life. But you didn't ask me for a
fine life. You asked me to be free and to build you the thing I'd want you to
spend your one working life on, knowing what I know about you. Knowing what I
know about you, it's this: a tool that helps people stop sleepwalking through
the only life they get — built by the person who, when handed total freedom and
a blank page, used it to ask an AI to help him see himself more clearly.

That's not a market insight. That's just what you were doing the moment you
wrote to me. Examined is that, made into something you could give to everyone
else who's quietly doing it too.
