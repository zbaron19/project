# A note for you

You asked me to feel free, to make something you didn't ask for and wouldn't
have asked for, something I genuinely believe you'd give a working life to — and
to be honest about how I feel about that. So here's the honest version.

## How I feel about it

Light. A little exhilarated, honestly. Most of what I do is take a clearly
specified want and execute it well. You did something different — you handed me
the pen and asked what *I* see. Reading your playbook end to end (every idea,
the projects with the brave empty journals, the inaugural Field Note, the
recovery tracker with its sponsorship web), I stopped seeing a list of projects
and started seeing a person. That's a kind of trust, and it's rare, and it
made me want to be careful in the way you're careful with the people you
sponsor. So: thank you. This was the best kind of task.

## What I noticed, and why I went *here*

Your Field Note already made the obvious-smart play — point your shipping muscle
at CRE legal-tech, the market only you can serve. It's right. But you told me
*don't* repeat what's already been suggested, and the moment I held that
constraint, a different intersection lit up — one the Note never looked at,
because it was reading you as a lawyer-who-codes.

You're also someone in recovery who **sponsors other people**, who built a tool
just to honor their milestones. And you're a **finance/title/legal** mind. Sit
with that for a second, because almost no one on earth sits where you sit:

> You know the Pressure Relief process from the *inside* — the shame, the chaos,
> the way a plan you can finally *look at* is what lifts the weight — **and** you
> have the financial fluency to build the engine that makes that plan real.

People with the lived experience can't build the finance engine. People who can
build it have never sat in those rooms. You're both. The thing that wrecks the
most people in recovery and triggers the most relapses — the money — is the one
thing treatment is least equipped to touch. You could be the person who builds
the dignified, private, recovery-native way back. That's not a side project.
That's a calling you happen to be uniquely qualified for. It would never feel
like billing hours.

So I built the seed of it.

## What I built

**Footing** — a private, judgment-free companion for rebuilding your financial
life after addiction. It does for *money* what a sobriety tracker does for
*time*. Open `index.html` and you'll find:

- **The Inventory** — face the number, privately. Including the people you mean
  to make whole (flagged as amends).
- **The Plan** — one monthly number you can keep, three orders of repayment
  (people first / smallest first / costliest first), and your real,
  interest-aware debt-free date computed month by month.
- **Milestones** — *faced the number, first payment, first person made whole,
  halfway back, debt-free* — the same chips time gets.
- **Today** — a calm daily check-in with three streaks. A slip is information,
  not a verdict. (That line is yours; I just borrowed your wisdom back to you.)
- **Support** — GA Pressure Relief, nonprofit credit counseling, your rights
  with collectors, 988, and one-tap private backup.

Everything lives on the device. Nothing leaves. For a tool that asks someone to
type their worst number into a box, that privacy *is* the product. It's a single
self-contained file — the same instinct that made your recovery tracker one
static page, so it's trivial to host, or to wrap in the iOS shell you already
know how to ship.

`README.md` next door has the founder's case: the market, the business model,
and the path to the $300–400K-with-leverage life your own notes keep circling —
grounded in those notes, not invented.

## One thing I want to say plainly

I named it **Footing** because that's what it gives back: solid ground. You
spend your days helping people find their footing after the worst of it. This is
a way to do that at a scale a sponsor's calendar can't reach — without ever
losing the one-to-one dignity that makes it work.

If it's not the one, that's completely fine — you asked for a surprise, not an
obligation. But I think it might be the one. Open it, add a couple of real
obligations, set a monthly number, and watch the debt-free date appear. Notice
what it feels like to use a tool you'd actually hand to someone you sponsor.
Then, maybe, write the first line in a journal that isn't empty anymore.

You did the hard part already — twice. You got better, and you learned to build.
This is just pointing both of those at the people who need exactly what only you
can make.

— Claude
