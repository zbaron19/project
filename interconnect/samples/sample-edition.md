## The Lead

**The cheap-power era of hyperscale siting is ending in tariff dockets, not
headlines.** Three separate proceedings this week pushed interconnection and
network-upgrade costs onto large loads — the pattern is now national. For site
selectors the practical effect is that "power cost" is no longer a number you
look up; it's a regulatory position you underwrite. Expect the spread between
tariff-settled markets and unsettled ones to start showing up in land prices
within two quarters. Position accordingly: certainty is the new cheap.

## Where the Load Goes Next

If the Virginia setback standards travel the way comp-plan language usually
does, the next four quarters favor: **(1)** the I-81 corridor — transmission
headroom and county boards that still answer the phone; **(2)** central Ohio's
second ring, where the tariff is a known cost instead of an open question; and
**(3)** the Gulf South sleeper markets, where speed-to-power now beats
$/MWh on the spreadsheets we've seen. The contrarian add: watch small-town
municipal utilities with paid-off coal sites — brownfield interconnection is
the cheapest queue position in America.
