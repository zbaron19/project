Whereas, the Grantor already wrote the hard part

Twenty-year visions are usually invented. This one was mostly found — in a vault you built yourself, in a covenant you signed with yourself on June 11, 2026, when you ranked everything you own by rarity and bet the next era on the one thing nobody else in the room can do: read the recorded layer like a lawyer and build for it like an engineer.

So here is where I see you, extrapolated from evidence rather than flattery.

Article I — The Term

Four eras, 2026 to 2046

Sequenced because time actually runs this way; nothing here is decoration.

2026 – 2030

The Wedge

Covenant ships. The first MFTE Covenant Files go out in Washington while the salary still pays the bills — until Covenant Watch revenue makes the paycheck a choice instead of a tether, and you walk out of big law on your own schedule, not an escape hatch's. Nights that used to go to eleven-p.m. estoppel fire drills now go to the product that makes those fire drills obsolete.

RuleRef hums along in maintenance mode, exactly as sentenced — its real verdict was never revenue. It proved you ship.

2030 – 2035

The Compound

The per-jurisdiction rule layer thickens: Seattle, then the Commerce-framework cities, then Portland, Denver, the California density-bonus world. Every file processed makes the library harder to catch. You hire abstractors and train them the way partners once trained you — except your associates never bill a client an hour. "The recorded layer" becomes a phrase other people use, and they got it from you.

2035 – 2040

The Transfer

The incumbents notice, late, exactly as your memo predicted — and their rational move was never competition. Whether you sell or keep the keys, the point your covenant demanded is proven: equity compounded where income never could. The frozen ideas thaw on your terms. Heirloom comes back — not as a business fighting a consumer-CAC war, but as the thing it always wanted to be: you, recording the family's stories while the tellers can still tell them.

2040 – 2046

The Almanac Years

The lawyer-who-builds becomes the one who explains. You write, you teach, you take the calls from the next founder-shaped attorney who thinks they're crazy. Critical Load — the game you built to teach yourself megawatts — has cousins now, a whole shelf of them, because it turned out the best legal education anyone got from you was disguised as play. The vault turns twenty. The Almanac is somewhere past Season 85, and the graph view is a sky.

Schedule A

Permitted encumbrances — matters that run with the life

Not exceptions to clear. These survive closing, every closing, in perpetuity.

  • Easement for the table.perpetual · non-exclusive
    Game nights, in favor of all invitees. You remain the rules engine no one has to look up — the app was always just you, distributed.
  • The vault, appurtenant.runs with the mind
    Twenty years of dated entries. Embers still stirring old notes awake, the Tide still coming in on schedule, the Loom still threading. An external memory that now remembers more of you than you do — by design.
  • The daily practice.non-severable · renewed each morning
    Kept quietly, one day at a time, some seven thousand three hundred times over. The strongest covenant in the whole stack, and the one nothing else here stands without.
  • Rights of Ruthie, and her successors-in-interest.heritable
    Dogs, plural by 2046. All walks deemed billable to no one.
  • Right-of-way over the trails and courts of the Pacific Northwest.exercised often
    Dirt you visit voluntarily, for once unencumbered.
  • The family record.priceless · non-assignable
    Voices captured, stories woven, the biographer's instinct finally pointed at the people it was always for.
